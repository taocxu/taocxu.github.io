  Publications

    "Towards Sustainable Prosperity? Policy Evaluation of Jiangsu Advanced Manufacturing Clusters,"
    with Yabei Hu,
    Technology in Society, 2024, 77, 102583 [PDF]

      The government of China, to hone manufacturing's competitive edge, has adopted a series of regional industrial policies to stimulate advanced manufacturing clusters as facilitators of agglomeration effects, utilisers of factor endowments, and implementers of innovation solutions, by playing the role of facilitating state. Focusing on advanced manufacturing cluster policies in East China's Jiangsu Province, the research employs a mixed data analysis methodology on a sample of 52 documents collected from the Jiangsu government and affiliated units. The policy orientations and priorities are examined under the triple analytical framework in terms of themes, instruments, and controls, applying textual mining and the PMC index model. The results reveal the alignment of policy themes with high-quality development strategy, the government preferences for the supply dimension in policy instruments, the emphasis on planning directive in policy controls, together with the relatively high internal consistency of the AMC policy toolkit by Jiangsu. Therefore, the research spotlights the importance of leveraging comparative strengths based upon the facilitating state institution, and of deploying internally coherent, scientific, and efficient initiatives that exploit more potential of advanced manufacturing clusters to foster sustainable prosperity from the perspective of new structural economics.

    "Common Prosperity and the Middle Class,"
    WHU Proceedings of the Summer Institute on Marxian Economics, 2023, 4, 1-5

      The realisation of ‘common prosperity’ constitutes a complex and long-term course. To achieve shared benefits and an inclusive society, a series of initiatives are required, including exploring and pursuing excellence in optimising the distribution of national wealth, refining the policy mechanisms, and adapting to digital transformation with technology education. Meanwhile, according to historical materialism, the conditions faced by the middle class, the evolving connotations to which they belong, and the social realities are constantly shifting along with the structural changes in the economy and society. Therefore, in-depth investigations of its role in the progress towards ‘common prosperity’ are urgent, drawing on the experience of the class struggles from the ‘Three Worlds’, the status quo in the West, and discussions aimed at deciphering and preventing the ‘middle-class trap’ in China's context.

    "Mismatch and Dysfunction in the Ageing Economy: Internet-Optimised Transformation of the Senior Market,"
    with Yukun Gu, Yansong Wang, and Qifei Chen,
    Journal of the Jiangsu Association for Commercial Economics, 2021, 38, 1-9 [PDF]

      With the endeavours towards healthy ageing, China’s older demographic exhibits characteristics of a substantial scale, a swift rate of increase, and uneven distribution between urban and rural areas. Social and economic development has elevated the material conditions and living standards of city seniors, directing our focus on the heightened demand and inequality from this demographic. However, significant deficiencies in the myopic supply strategy of China’s pro-senior consumer market precipitate conspicuous mismatch and dysfunction. The mismatch and disparity between supply and demand feature increasingly manifest, with the market inadequately positioning the genuine needs of seniors across heterogeneous regions. Consequently, the emerging undersupply of the silver-hair industry necessitates technoscience-based improvement. 1) The economies of scale and the scalability of the Internet are expanding the pro-senior market, thus facilitating industrial development with digital transformation. 2) Then, leveraging big data mitigates the information asymmetry between the supply and demand perspectives. 3) Furthermore, e-commerce enables deeper, sustainable production and consumption, while 4) intelligent logistics handle the ‘last mile’ challenge in both urban-rural supply chains. Collectively, the digital synergy creates inclusive opportunities for optimisation, delivering our Internet-based pathway to rectify market mismatch and dysfunction.

    "Reshaping Silver-Hair Industry with New Retailing: A SWOT-Based Strategy,"
    Journal of the China Federation of Logistics, 2021, 28, 82-85 [PDF]

      The silver-hair industry, a sector that grasps the consumption patterns of the older population and welfare benefits for seniors, is being increasingly highlighted by an ageing society. Augmented by socio-economic development and the integration of digital technology, the silver-hair industry in China grapples with an imperative need for digital transformation and a people-first marketing strategy. New retailing is revolutionising business models, enhancing the retail environment, and altering supplier-consumer dynamics. By integrating online and offline platforms with logistics, new retailing fortifies the entire industrial chains. Extensive data collection and analytics enable the optimisation of product design and consumer management, not only satisfying the diverse needs of seniors but also transforming the outdated processes of production and distribution within the silver-hair industry. This strategy amplifies the potential of the ‘silver-hair economy’ by bringing diversification, experiential offerings, cultural engagement, and social interaction. Through our SWOT-based strategy, this research elucidates the prospects of new retailing within this sector, delineating the opportunities and challenges in business praxis.
