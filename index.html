ABOUT TAO

An upcoming PhD student of development economics at SOAS. Graduating with the highest distinction from NUFE and a merit from the University of Edinburgh, preparing my doctoral research in the political economy of development on the micro-foundations of industrial policy and political settlements from ideological (Žižekian) and institutional (evolutionary) perspectives. Proficient in NVivo, Stata, ArcMap, Python, Matlab, and open to both qualitative and quantitative methodologies, with extensive industrial and academic experience at CITIC, Mazars, Deloitte, HQSW, Duke Kunshan, NUFE, UoEdin, UoSheff, HNU, SUFE, Fudan, Bayes, several public and private organisations. Involved in a range of heterodox and evidence-based economic research such as the political economy of urban & international development, political settlements, informal institutions, industrial policy, transport infrastructure policy, and entrepreneurship alike, with an interest in the most and least represented local samples across diverse formal & informal institutions. Seeking further professional challenges and mission-oriented, policy-relevant academic environments at international research universities where methodological and theoretical pluralism is valued to apply my knowledge of real-world economics and a transferable skill set.

PUBLICATIONS

  1. ‘Towards Sustainable Prosperity? Policy Evaluation of Jiangsu Advanced Manufacturing Clusters’

    with Yabei Hu, Technology in Society, 2024, 77, 102583, honoured by Jiangsu Provincial Distinguished Award  ·  [PDF]

    This research delivers a multifaceted evaluation of Jiangsu’s selective, meticulous industrial policy of development and innovation for sustainable prosperity.

    • Policy Text Evaluation: Examines 52 policy documents with a mixed analytical framework.
    • Strategic Focus: Interprets East China’s transition to a global manufacturing powerhouse, spotlighting industrial ecosystem-oriented development.
    • Socioeconomic Development: Details Jiangsu’s phased progress in cultivating industrial clusters and sustaining regional economic prosperity.

    The findings portray a blueprint for leveraging advanced manufacturing clustering to achieve regional sustainable growth. The research makes a constructive contribution by encouraging future work to investigate the evolving impact and critically analyse the sustainability outcomes of a revived but distinctive industrial policy toolkit amid global economic and socio-political shifts.
