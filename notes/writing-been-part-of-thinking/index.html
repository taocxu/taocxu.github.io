Writing’s Been Part of Thinking

21 August 2026 Tao Louie Xu

Writing’s been part of thinking. Wrote a sentence, then judged it, revised it. This is unchanged.

What may change is the first reader. Perhaps no longer ourselves, our colleagues or a supervisor. AI is. We judge AI’s suggestions. Pick this one? Reject that? How much to take?

I suppose writing down our own ideas and judgements remains indispensable. Good ideas and good judgements hinge on academic taste, experience, knowledge, and perspectives. We have. AI ‘may know’ a great deal but doesn’t necessarily know ‘what it knows’ for a particular section we’re working on. We know. We have to tell it.

The ‘telling’ per se is writing and thinking. We can also use a word AI loves to use: navigate.

If we human scholars know the direction, even with a vague sense of what we want at the beginning, AI could give what we need and we will make it clear. If we do not, AI easily pretends to be omniscient and leaves users dizzy.

I saw students begin by asking AI to give research questions and propositions before clarifying their own ideas and judgements. Behind weak sentences were weak ideas and weak judgements.

I think the key boundary is less about who wrote each sentence, and more about the ideas, judgements, and the direction we are telling AI to go with us.
